## Origin

Talia was born to scholars — a historian-warrior mother and a philosopher father. Her family held a small home in a quiet village, and her earliest memories are of her mother's maps and her father's books, and the sense that her parents saw patterns other people did not.

The patterns they saw were real. A cult had come to the village — quiet, robed in gray, speaking of peace and surrender and release from earthly attachment. They called themselves Children of the Ash, and Talia's parents were among the first to recognize what the cult was actually doing: hunting.

What Children of the Ash was hunting for was the blood in Talia's veins.

Her parents died protecting a secret they never had time to explain to her. She found their bodies laid in perfect circles inside the cult's compound, arranged like offerings. She was barely more than a child.

## The Phoenix's Ghost

What came next is not a story the Keeper often tells of her own volition. It is preserved in the chronicles for the same reason all hard things are preserved: redemption cannot begin where truth is hidden.

After the murders, Talia ran. She ran until she was far enough from home that no one would know her name, and then she made a vow: never again would she be vulnerable. Never again would she arrive too late.

She found a weapons master in a border town — a grizzled veteran who asked no questions about the desperate girl with fury in her eyes. He offered training in exchange for years of service. She accepted without hesitation. The training was brutal and the service was harder, and she did both without complaint, without explanation, and without speaking once of the nightmares that woke her in the night. Her master noticed. He was wise enough to say nothing, and simply made her stronger.

When her debt was paid and he released her, she was ready.

The Children of the Ash had not been idle in her absence. They no longer wore gray robes or gathered in compounds. They had learned subtlety — holding normal jobs, wearing ordinary faces, blending into the community like rot beneath clean wood. They had claimed her family home as their headquarters.

Talia walked her hometown's streets and no one recognized her. She spent weeks watching. Learning. Identifying every member of the cult, mapping their movements, understanding where they felt safe. They had grown comfortable in years of unchallenged control. They had stopped watching for threats.

That was their mistake.

She moved through her family home like smoke — around the cult members sleeping in her childhood bedroom, past those praying in her father's study — until she found, in the basement, her mother's armor. Red and black leather. Battle-worn and magnificent. She put it on, and it fit perfectly, as if it had been waiting.

Then she went to work.

It took years to destroy them completely. She uncovered them one by one — followed their movements, learned their secrets, and when she had proof beyond doubt, she struck. Sometimes in dark alleys. Sometimes in their homes. Always decisively. Always final. She never killed the innocent. She showed no mercy to the guilty. The cult began to fear the woman in red and black leather and gave her a name: The Phoenix's Ghost. They did not know she was the child who had escaped them, returned as their reckoning.

As their numbers dwindled, they grew desperate. They fled. She found them. They hid. She waited. They fought. She was better.

The last of them died on a cold night, years after her parents' murders. Their final compound burned — full circle. They had killed her parents in fire and ceremony. She returned the favor.

She stood in the center of the inferno and felt the heat that should have killed her, the smoke that should have choked her, the collapsing building that should have buried her.

Nothing touched her.

The flames parted around her like water around stone. Talia looked at her hands and saw them trailing fire — not burning. Becoming fire. The memories came then, in fragments: childhood incidents, candles that never burned her fingers, her mother's knowing looks, her father's careful questions about how she felt near flame. The reason the Children of the Ash had targeted her family — specifically, deliberately — for something she hadn't known she possessed.

She whispered to the ash: "They were hunting me because I'm touched by Ignus. And you —" she looked toward her parents' distant grave. "You died protecting that secret."

She walked out of the burning compound unmarked. The town discovered what she had done within days. Some people were relieved. Most were horrified. No one thanked her. No one invited her to stay. No one looked at her with anything but fear, or judgment, or uncomfortable relief.

Talia stood in the town square in her mother's armor and felt nothing. They were alive. The rot was dead. The cult that had murdered her parents could never hurt anyone again.

That had to be enough.

She was Emberborn. Touched by Ignus. Fire made flesh.

## The Wandering, and the Six Strangers

She left that same night. Walked away from the only home she had ever known, from the grave where her parents lay finally avenged, from a town full of people who couldn't look her in the eye.

She did not know she was gathering doctrine. She thought she was just surviving.

Kira found her on a merchant caravan, working as a guard — the only work available to a woman with her particular skillset and no desire to explain herself. Kira was the merchant's daughter, quick-handed and quick-laughed, and she talked constantly into the silence Talia had grown accustomed to. You're scared of connection, she said one evening, with the unnerving accuracy of someone who has nothing to lose by being honest. Like if you let people in, they'll take something from you. When bandits struck the caravan three days later, Talia fought with precision while Kira moved through the chaos behind her — organizing the merchants, getting them to safety, turning panic into coordinated retreat. Afterward, as they tended the wounded, Kira said: You fight. I organize. Separately we're good. Together we're better. Talia thought about that long after the caravan reached its destination and she moved on.

Lesson: Collective power emerges from genuine cooperation.

A healer with hands gnarled by arthritis but eyes that saw everything gave Talia a bed in exchange for guard work and proceeded to charge an entirely different toll. You carry pain like armor, she said one evening, not looking up from her work. Wear it long enough, and you forget what your skin feels like without it. Talia watched her for weeks — how she moved through rooms of the broken and the bleeding, how she created safety through presence alone. No weapons. No threats. Just competent hands and a voice that said: you're safe here. I've got you. The lesson didn't feel like a lesson. It felt like an observation Talia couldn't stop thinking about.

Lesson: Sanctuary is created through genuine presence, not through force.

A farmer gave her a harvest season of honest work and almost no conversation, which suited her well. On the evening before she planned to leave, he told her what his late wife used to say: that bitterness is just sweetness that's been squeezed wrong. Same fruit. Different choice. The morning she left, his daughter pressed a preserved lemon into her hand. Talia carried it for months — not as sentiment, but as a reminder. When it finally crumbled to nothing in her pack, the idea remained. It became the seed of what the Keepers would one day call the Philosophy of the Lemon: the understanding that sourness and sweetness are the same substance, and that what you make of adversity is always, always a choice.

Lesson: Adversity can be transformed through intentional action.

A mercenary captain told her, bluntly and without apology, that she fought like someone who didn't care whether she survived. Warriors who've lost everything fight with nothing to lose. You know what happens to them? They either die, or they find something worth living for. No middle ground. Talia had no answer. But she stayed with the company long enough to watch how soldiers function — not as individuals but as a unit, covering weaknesses and amplifying strengths, trusting implicitly because their lives required it. One night after a successful mission, someone started singing around the fire. Others joined. The sound built into something larger than any single voice. Talia didn't sing. She listened. And felt something stir in her chest that wasn't pain or anger or the particular emptiness she had been carrying for years.

Lesson: When individuals gather with shared purpose, they create something greater than the sum of their parts.

A philosophy scholar in a university town hired her as a combat instructor and spent every lesson asking why. Why this grip and not that one? At first the questions irritated her. Slowly she began to understand what he was doing — looking for the principle beneath the technique, the truth beneath the form. You're teaching us how to transform disadvantage into advantage, he said eventually. How to take what looks like weakness and make it strength. She thought, later, that she had been teaching herself the same lesson for years without knowing it.

Lesson: Transformation requires pressure. Adversity is the force that reshapes us.

An abbot at a monastery gave her shelter and very little else, except one conversation that stayed. You confuse identity with action, he said. You're not a warrior because you fight. You fight because of who you are beneath the armor. The question is: who is that? Who were you before violence? Who might you be after? Talia had no answer. But in the silence the monastery required of its guests, she began to hear something she hadn't heard in years — a rhythm beneath her thoughts. A current beneath her fear. Something that had always been there, waiting for her to be still enough to notice.

Lesson: Peace isn't the absence of struggle. It's the presence of something stronger than struggle.

By the time she walked into the mists of Hynafol, she was carrying her mother's armor, her people's blood, and six stranger-lessons she had not yet learned how to plant.

## Arrival in Hynafol

After seasons of wandering, Talia sat by a river and took inventory of what she had gathered. She had six lessons she didn't yet know how to assemble. She had her mother's armor and a blade that had tasted too much blood. She had the particular exhaustion of someone who has spent years becoming a weapon and is now entirely uncertain what else to be.

She was done running. She was not done becoming.

The mists found her at the edge of the known world. She'd heard stories of Hynafol in taverns and markets — the land beyond the veil, where old magic stirred and people went to start over. She stood at the boundary and felt something on the other side lean toward her.

She walked in without hesitating.

The fire woke immediately and completely. Not the destructive flame of the burning compound — something older. Primal. Elemental. It moved through her veins like molten gold and ignited nerves she hadn't known existed. She stumbled, pressed her hands to her face, and when she pulled them away they were trailing smoke. Small flames danced across her fingers.

She laughed. Wild and a little broken. And let them burn.

She emerged from the mists unmarked, fire moving freely across her hands, understanding at last what the Children of the Ash had known before she did and what her parents had died to protect: she was Emberborn. Touched by Ignus. Fire made flesh.

Talia herself stands with the Court of Valor — drawn there by her commitment to honor, justice, and the keeping of one's word. The Keepers of the Current are an independent faith. Members are free to join any House, Court, Kingdom, or guild. Their chosen affiliations do not affect their place in the Circle, and no member's allegiance binds the faith or its other members. Talia's standing in Valor is her personal choice.

Shortly after her arrival, a scholar rediscovered the manuscript that would give her a name for what she had been carrying all her life. (The manuscript had sat miscatalogued for longer than anyone could say, shelved in the cooking section of the Library of House Menatu Vandor because its cover bore a single, unmistakable lemon. What it contained was not a recipe. It was a doctrine. Talia recognized every word of it, because she had spent the last several years living it. See: The Philosophy of the Lemon.)

She had not set out to found a faith. She had set out to stop being a weapon.

The Current, apparently, had other ideas.

She has been planting the strangers' lessons ever since.

## The Coin of the Keepers

The first works of the Current under Talia's hand were quiet — small coins of bronze and knotwork pressed into the palms of souls who had recognized the faith without being asked to. She refused (and refuses) to recruit by conversion. She gives the Coin only in recognition. Many bear it now.

## The Working for Ophelia's Heart (Grand Gathering, November 2025)



The first widely witnessed working of the Keepers of the Current — and the first time the world saw the Keeper's fire — was the working for the soul known as Ophelia, whose heart had been taken by the entity Sin. Sin had possessed the Last Unicorn and used the creature as her vessel.

Willow, who had spent weeks studying the possession in hope of saving the Unicorn without destroying the entity within it, came to Talia and asked her to invoke the Circle of Truth. The Keeper consented. Several souls were drawn into the Circle: Willow, BearClaw, Sin (through the Unicorn), and others bound to the unfolding crisis.

Inside the Circle, Willow questioned Sin's nature and the conditions of Ophelia's possession. Sin answered, compelled to honesty, but searched for loopholes even where lies were forbidden. Then BearClaw asked whether Sin would honor the bargain the two of them had struck. Sin smiled, and said yes — and the Current warned the Keeper of a wrongness in that smile.

Compelled by the same Circle, BearClaw spoke the bargain aloud: the return of Ophelia's heart, in exchange for the death of the King.

Talia did not move to stop BearClaw. It was not her place to pass moral judgment on a vow already made. She did, however, warn the King that an attempt would be made on his life.

That night, BearClaw returned to the Keeper devastated. The deed is done. But Sin will not give the heart back.

The Keeper made her choice. The vow had been spoken inside the Circle of Truth, where words bind. To allow Sin to break it would be to allow the Current itself to be broken. Vows matter. Promises create bonds. Words spoken with intention bind us to something greater than ourselves.

She told BearClaw to bring the Unicorn and Ophelia to her so the broken vow could be confronted.

When the Unicorn arrived, Sin was contained by a binding collar that suppressed her control. The Keeper made an instruction that startled most who witnessed it: she had the collar removed. Sin had to be free to be confronted, and the broken vow had to be enforced against the entity itself. Those gathered stood ready in a circle of steel around the Unicorn, weapons drawn, surrounding the creature from every direction. A circle of steel and shared resolve.

The collar came off. The Unicorn's eyes shifted. Sin took full control.

The Keeper named the vow back to her, exact: If I kill the King, would YOU give the heart back. Sin claimed she no longer held the heart. At that moment, Sin's keeper — the one who held the heart in his physical possession — entered the Circle. He attempted to open new bargains. The Keeper cut him off and explained, plainly, the cosmic consequence of allowing a lie to stand inside a Circle of Truth. He surrendered the heart.

The heart was blackened, pulsing with corruption. Sin had held it too long. To return it as it was would have killed Ophelia, or worse.

What happened next was the first time the Keeper showed her fire to the world.

A voice in the gathered crowd called out, protect the ritual, and a second circle of guards formed around the Keeper without anyone needing to be asked twice. The Current was already moving through them. Talia opened every channel of her body and let the fire come — fed by the unified intention of every soul standing with her, stronger than she had ever felt it before.

She held the corrupted heart and forced the fire through it. Hot enough to burn away Sin's taint. Restrained enough not to destroy what she was trying to save. When the work was done, the heart was clean. The last traces of Sin fled the flame.

She placed the heart back into Ophelia's chest. Ophelia remarked at once on the heat, the heartburn, and the hot flushes — and then, life returned.

Some of that fire passed through the Keeper into the heart and stayed there. The cost is hers to carry. Whether it fades or leaves a mark is something only time will answer.

This is the working that brought BearClaw into the company of the Keepers, and it is the working that revealed the Keeper's Emberborn nature publicly for the first time. It is also remembered as the defining doctrinal act of the Keepers of the Current: the Circle is opened to the one who seeks to return, never closed against him.

## The Keeper's Beacon (February 2026)

The first Keeper's Beacon was lit in February 2026: a torch kindled in the center of the Circle, a guiding fire for any soul seeking their way home.

For Talia, the Beacon speaks to the work she carries as Keeper — tending the Current, safeguarding its sacred objects, and making space for honest connection. Its flame marks the Circle wherever the Keepers gather. Any soul who approaches in good faith may be witnessed there.

## The Unity Mead (March 2026)

At the Trials expedition in March of 2026, Talia stood as the High Priestess of the largest multi-faith working the Keepers have yet performed. Under the hospitality of the Keepers of the Current, twenty banners, faiths, kingdoms, and individuals gave honey, spirit, and offering into a sanctified brewing. Each contribution was individually blessed by the High Priestess's hand.

The working is remembered for three covenants named that night: The Weight of Many, The Current's Warning, and The Covenant's Memory. Each offering became part of a shared working, witnessed by those who gathered.

The ritual was sanctified by allied clergy of Cliona, Mascarino, the Old Ways, Eluna, Invictus, Caernos, the Creed of Air and Darkness, and Velt Ester. The Keepers' own Priest Lysus Maowulf rose to priesthood at that gathering and performed his first rite.

Twice that same evening, unknown hands attempted to break the ritual circle from outside. The circle held.

## The Assassination of the Keeper

On the day of the Unity Mead — the Trials expedition, March 2026 — before the working began, Talia Emberborn was assassinated.

The strike came from Anya, working as a contracted hand for an employer who has not yet been named. She moved on Talia while the Keeper stood beside Ronan the Barbarian in a common gathering space. Aldrif Corswain held a full view of the incident and gave the witnessing account that was entered at the Archives.

The Keeper fell. One of her six lives was registered spent at the Archives that day.

Phoenix Reborn. She rose — fire makes fire, and an Emberborn does not hold the last word in her own death easily — and stood for the Unity Mead that evening, because the working did not wait for her convenience and she did not ask it to. The Circle opened. The offerings were blessed. The three covenants were bound.

One question from the assassination remains unresolved:

• The employer is unknown. Anya struck under contract, not banner. Who paid the contract has not been answered. The Current remembers the hand, but the hand was hired.

• Phoenix Reborn — earned through the fires she has walked out of unmarked, and the deaths she has returned from
• High Priestess — her clergy rank within the Keepers of the Current
• The Phoenix's Ghost — a name from before Hynafol, held by the Keeper as memory rather than title

## Relationships

Where the Keeper's work is to draw all things together, a Witcher's is to stand alone against what should not be; where Valor seeks the keeping of the law, Power seeks what lies beneath it. By every Court's reckoning, the two of them should orbit and pass. They have not. He has sworn his sword to her protection, and in return the Keeper has gifted him a blessed bow that he now carries onto the field of battle. What runs between them beyond the oath has been observed by those around them and named by neither of them. They circle still. The distance between them has narrowed.
